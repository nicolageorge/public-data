# Detailed Report: Public and Concertado Schools in Málaga, Spain for a Family with One Child

## 1. Executive overview

For a family moving to, or choosing schools within, Málaga city, the key decision is not simply “which school ranks highest,” because Spain and Andalucía do not publish a single official league table comparable to UK-style school performance tables. Instead, school choice in Málaga must be based on several overlapping factors:

- **Admission zone / área de influencia**, especially for public and concertado schools.
- **Official bilingual status**, best verified through the Junta de Andalucía.
- **Reputation and family demand**, visible through platforms such as Micole, Escolia, Buscocoles and local press.
- **Educational continuity**, i.e. whether the school offers Infantil, Primaria, ESO and Bachillerato.
- **English provision**, including official bilingual programme, Cambridge preparation, Trinity, BEDA or other language frameworks.
- **Costs**, particularly comedor, aula matinal, extraescolares, uniforms, AMPA and voluntary contributions in concertado schools.
- **Religious character**, especially for concertados, many of which in Málaga are Catholic, though there are also laic/cooperative options.

The best answer for most families is to create a shortlist by neighbourhood, then verify current data directly with each school and through the Junta’s escolarización portal before applying.

---

## 2. How school admissions work in Málaga

Public and concertado schools in Andalucía use the official **Junta de Andalucía escolarización process**. For Infantil, Primaria, ESO and Bachillerato, families generally apply during March for the following academic year. For 2026/27, the ordinary application period was **1–31 March 2026**.

Admission priority is strongly influenced by the family’s home address or workplace, through the official **áreas de influencia**. These catchment zones are updated annually and published by the Junta. This matters because even an excellent school may not be a realistic option if the family does not score enough proximity points.

Concertado schools are privately owned but publicly funded for certain stages. Admission to concerted stages follows the same broad process as public schools. A school may be concertado for Infantil, Primaria and ESO but private for Bachillerato.

## 3. On rankings and academic results

There is no official comprehensive ranking of Málaga schools by academic performance. Available rankings are mostly produced by private portals or newspapers. They are useful as signals, but should not be treated as definitive.

### Main ranking/reference sources

| Source | Usefulness | Limitation |
|---|---:|---|
| **Junta de Andalucía** | Official for school status, bilingual authorisation, address, admission zones | Does not publish simple public rankings by school |
| **Micole** | Useful for family ratings, popularity, claimed academic data | Methodology combines metrics, user reviews and submitted data |
| **Escolia** | Useful for estimated fees, stages, ownership and filters | Estimates may not equal official school prices |
| **El Mundo ranking** | National reputation signal, especially for private/concertado schools | Less useful for public schools; methodology favours visible/private schools |
| **Local press** | Good for awards, PAU highlights, news | Usually anecdotal or based on individual success stories |

Andalucía does run **Pruebas de Evaluación de Diagnóstico** in 4.º Primaria and 2.º ESO, but results are primarily for internal improvement and are not published as a public league table.

## 4. English curriculum and Cambridge: important distinction

Families often ask whether a school is “Cambridge accredited,” but there are several different meanings:

1. **Official bilingual school**: authorised by the Junta to teach part of the curriculum in English, French or German.
2. **Cambridge English Preparation Centre**: prepares pupils for Cambridge English exams such as Starters, Movers, Flyers, A2 Key, B1 Preliminary, B2 First or C1 Advanced.
3. **Cambridge English Authorised Exam Centre**: administers Cambridge English exams. This is a stronger formal status than being a preparation centre.
4. **Cambridge International School**: registered to offer Cambridge International curriculum/exams such as IGCSE and A Levels.

Cambridge English itself notes that “Centro Preparador Oficial” is not the same as an authorised exam centre; preparation centres are independent and not quality-controlled in the same way as authorised exam centres. Therefore, when a Málaga school says it prepares Cambridge exams, ask:

- Which exams? Young Learners, B1, B2, C1?
- Are exams taken on-site or through an external academy?
- Is the school an exam centre or only a preparation centre?
- Are Cambridge classes included in the timetable or paid extraescolar?
- What percentage of pupils sit the exams?

# 6. Recommended concertado schools in Málaga

Concertado schools can be very attractive because they combine publicly funded education with private management, often with stronger identity, extended services, uniforms, religious ethos and sometimes more structured language programmes.

Legally, concertado schools **cannot charge mandatory tuition** for concerted teaching. However, families often pay for optional or complementary items: comedor, aula matinal, AMPA, uniforms, activities, insurance, digital platforms, voluntary foundation contributions and exam-preparation classes.

# 7. Cost guide and simulations for one child

## 7.1 Public school costs

Teaching is free. Official public service prices from Junta references for 2026/27 include:

- **Aula matinal:** €23.91/month or €1.82/day
- **Comedor escolar:** €5.54/day
- **Extraescolares:** €17.51/month per activity

There may be discounts or full subsidies depending on income and family circumstances.

### Public school monthly simulation: one child

Assume one child uses aula matinal, comedor 20 days/month and one extraescolar.

| Item | Estimated monthly cost |
|---|---:|
| Teaching | €0 |
| Aula matinal | €23.91 |
| Comedor, 20 days x €5.54 | €110.80 |
| One extraescolar | €17.51 |
| AMPA/material average estimate | €5–15 |
| **Estimated monthly total** | **€157–167** |

If the child does not use comedor or aula matinal, monthly cost may be very low, perhaps only materials/AMPA and occasional excursions.

## 7.2 Concertado school costs

Concerted teaching should be free, but real family spending can vary. Common cost items:

- Voluntary foundation or school contribution
- AMPA
- Uniform and sports kit
- Comedor
- Aula matinal
- Transport route
- Digital platform or device
- Insurance
- Extraescolares
- Cambridge/Trinity preparation
- Exam fees

Private studies cited in public debate estimate average base concertado contributions in Andalucía around **€28.60/month**, but this is only a general estimate and may be zero in some schools or higher in others depending on services.

### Concertado low-use simulation: one child

| Item | Estimated monthly cost |
|---|---:|
| Concerted teaching | €0 |
| Voluntary contribution/AMPA | €20–40 |
| No comedor, no aula matinal | €0 |
| One activity or English club | €20–60 |
| Uniform amortised monthly | €15–30 |
| **Estimated monthly total** | **€55–130** |

### Concertado full-services simulation: one child

| Item | Estimated monthly cost |
|---|---:|
| Concerted teaching | €0 |
| Voluntary contribution/AMPA | €20–60 |
| Comedor | €110–150 |
| Aula matinal | €25–40 |
| English Cambridge extraescolar | €50–70 |
| One sport/activity | €20–40 |
| Uniform/material/digital amortised | €20–40 |
| **Estimated monthly total** | **€245–400** |

### Example: La Presentación English extraescolar

Located in Pedregalejo, La Presentación has a documented English extraescolar example through Simona Learning:

- Registration: **€20 per family**
- Monthly English: **€58 for AMPA pupils**, **€62 without AMPA**
- Second sibling discounts were referenced, though this family has one child.

A realistic month with comedor and Cambridge-style English could look like:

| Item | Cost |
|---|---:|
| Voluntary/AMPA estimate | €25 |
| Comedor estimate | €120 |
| English extraescolar | €58 |
| Uniform/material amortised | €25 |
| **Total** | **~€228/month** |

# 10. Questions to ask every school before applying

1. Is the school officially bilingual according to the Junta? In which language and stages?
2. Which subjects are taught in English?
3. Are there native or conversation assistants?
4. Does the school prepare Cambridge English exams? Which levels?
5. Is Cambridge preparation included or paid extra?
6. Is the school an exam centre, or are exams taken elsewhere?
7. What were last year’s B1/B2/C1 pass rates?
8. What are the exact monthly costs for one child?
9. Are contributions voluntary? Can families opt out without consequences?
10. What is the comedor price and who operates it?
11. Are uniforms required? What is the first-year cost?
12. Is Bachillerato concertado or private?
13. Which secondary school is assigned if the centre only offers Primaria?
14. How many applications were received last year for available places?
15. What support exists for foreign/new Spanish-speaking pupils?

# 11. Final recommendation

For an English-focused family with one child, the best school depends heavily on where you live. If you want the lowest-cost route, choose a strong public English-bilingual CEIP in your catchment, with **CEIP Ciudad de Popayán**, **CEIP Pintor Félix Revello de Toro** and **CEIP Salvador Allende** as strong starting points.

If you want a concertado with identity, structure and longer continuity, the best Catholic options to visit first are **Maristas**, **San Estanislao**, **San José**, **La Presentación** and **El Divino Pastor**. For laic concertado options, focus on **Gibraljaire**, **El Limonar** and **El Atabal**, while verifying official bilingual status and costs.

For Cambridge specifically, do not rely only on marketing terms. Ask for current documentation showing whether the school is a Cambridge English preparation centre, authorised exam centre, or merely offers English classes aligned to Cambridge exams. For true Cambridge International curriculum such as IGCSE/A Levels, families should also consider international schools such as The British School of Málaga or other Cambridge International registered schools, though these are private and much more expensive than public or concertado schools.
